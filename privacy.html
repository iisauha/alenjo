Privacy

Last updated November 20, 2026

What Alenjo stores, where it’s stored, and which outside services see anything.

What’s stored

  • Your profile: name, email, and a profile photo if you add one.
  • Accounts: name, type, last 4 digits and balance for each account you connect.
  • Transactions: store, amount, swipe and post dates, category, and Plaid’s store logo and city.
  • Your edits: notes, renames, adjusted amounts, splits, moved dates, categories, logos you picked, and transactions you added yourself.
  • Card details when your bank shares them: APR, minimum payment, due date, statement balance.
  • Investments when your brokerage shares them: ticker, shares, value, cost.
  • Card design images you upload.

What’s not stored

  • Your bank username or password. You type those into Plaid’s window, not Alenjo’s.
  • Your face. Face ID runs on your phone; Alenjo only keeps a public key (see Security).
  • Full card or account numbers. Plaid only gives the last 4.

Where it lives

  • A Supabase Postgres database on AWS in Northern Virginia (us-east-1).
  • Account names, last 4 digits, balances and bank keys are encrypted inside the database, on top of Supabase’s disk encryption.
  • Photos and card images are in private storage. The app gets a link that expires after 7 days and only works for your own files.
  • On your device, only settings: theme, Face ID and auto-lock choices, the last tab you used, and your email and first name for the sign-in greeting. No balances or transactions.

Who else sees anything

  • Plaid: connects to your bank, read-only. Alenjo uses Transactions, Liabilities and Investments. It doesn’t use Plaid’s money-movement products, so it can’t move money.
  • Supabase: hosts the database, sign-in and the server code.
  • GitHub Pages: serves the app’s files. It sees your IP address like any website host.
  • logo.dev: gets a store’s website or name to return its logo, plus whatever you type in the logo search. Not your amounts or account.
  • Google Fonts: serves the two typefaces, so Google can see your IP address.
  • jsDelivr: only when you upload a card image, it serves a small model that checks the image on your phone. The image itself isn’t sent anywhere for that check.
  • No analytics, ad or tracking scripts are loaded. Nothing is sold or shared for marketing.

Deleting

  • One bank: Settings → Bank connections → Disconnect. Its accounts, transactions and your edits to them are deleted, and Plaid is told to revoke access.
  • Everything: Settings → Delete account. Every bank is disconnected at Plaid, then your data, photos and sign-in are deleted. This can’t be undone.
